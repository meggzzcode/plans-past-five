# R3-2 Creative Hobby Buyer review (simulated)

NOTE: These are simulated reviewer opinions from a persona (31-year-old, wants a creative hobby, will buy supplies, wary of affiliate blogs and AI filler). They are not real customer data. Tested live at http://127.0.0.1:5950, desktop 1280x800 and a brief pass at 390x844. I did not click through to Amazon or buy anything; I only read link targets.

## Task 1: pick a creative hobby and decide to try it - DONE
- Home: warm, photo-led hero ("Because the best part of your day is still ahead"), strong CTA "Find your thing". The "Creative Corner" tab in the sub-nav took me straight to a page of 6 hobbies (Watercolor, Air-Dry Clay, Crochet, Junk Journaling, Embroidery, Calligraphy), each with time, "Under $25/$50 to start" and energy level. Took about 3 clicks and under a minute.
- I chose junk journaling (also read watercolor and air-dry clay). The line "Empty your bag and wallet, pick three bits of paper from this week, and make one page about it" made me want to try it tonight. That is what convinced me.
- Oddity: on the Creative Corner grid, Crochet, Embroidery and Calligraphy have no photo, just a yellow tile with the name in script, so the grid looks half-finished next to Watercolor/Clay/Junk Journal, which have real images.

## Task 2: read the matching article - DONE
Read "How to Start Junk Journaling: A Beginner's Guide" (reached via the hobby page's Read Next / the Journal; my first guessed URL 404'd, the 404 page is friendly), plus the supplies guide and the "21 Creative Hobbies" list.
- Feels like a person: "Somewhere in your bag right now is the start of a very pretty page: a ticket stub, a coffee sleeve..." concrete, specific. A 6-step "first 30 minutes", four techniques (three colors, tear don't cut, pockets, tip-ins), and a "Common worries" section (messy pages, wrinkling, what to write) that answers what a nervous beginner actually thinks.
- Explains how to begin: yes, thoroughly, and with what I already own (notebook, glue stick, scissors, scraps).
- Supplies feel natural: very. Order is "start with what you have", then "if you'd like the pretty stuff, these are the four things worth buying first". The supplies guide says "make one page from only what you own, and note the one thing you truly wished you had. That is your first purchase." That is the opposite of an affiliate blog and the strongest trust moment on the site.
- Weak spots: the byline is "The Plans Past Five Editors", with no named person, no "I tried this" voice, no photos of a finished page by a real maker. Some listicle prose in the 21-hobbies article ("Consider this your reason", "oddly soothing") is a bit generic, and it repeats the same "oddly soothing" for calligraphy on cards. Several guides carry the same date (Nov 18/19, 2026), so the archive looks freshly generated, not lived in. The 21-hobbies article ends with shopping cards that are search links ("Find watercolor starter set on Amazon") while the hobby page names a specific product for the same item, an inconsistency.
- Some sections appear as headings with no visible body in the text view ("How your first junk journaling session goes", "Who this is for" on shop pages); I could not tell whether they are collapsed accordions. Not verified.

## Task 3: find the kit, pick the one thing, would I click - DONE
- Path: hobby page "See what you need" jumps to an on-page kit section: "If you only buy one thing, buy this".
- Junk journaling: one pick = "A5 hardcover journal, 120 gsm" (Stationery Island), reasoning "Thicker paper than a standard notebook, per the listing". Then "Scotch glue sticks, 4-pack". Extras clearly labelled "Nice later": floral washi (21 rolls), cottagecore ephemera pack (200 pieces), Pigma Micron pens.
- I would click the journal button: the reason is a real criterion (paper weight, which the supplies guide explains buckles with glue), it's cheap-feeling, and the site honestly says I could use an old notebook. I would NOT click the 200-piece "cottagecore ephemera pack"; it contradicts the "paper with a past" philosophy and feels like filler, though it is correctly marked "later".
- Link target: "See it on Amazon" opens https://www.amazon.com/dp/B081VK1CM1 in a new tab (target=_blank rel=noopener), a named product page, not a search. Watercolor pick B0BWRCKFDH, clay kit B0DFH9Z3BC, etc. Where the site only describes a type, the button reads "Find ... on Amazon" and goes to an amazon.com/s?k= search. The labels match the destinations.
- Caveats: no prices shown (I would have to leave the site to learn the cost; the "$0 to $20" band on the hobby page helps). No ratings or review counts, so I can't judge quality without clicking. "Prefer something different? (1)" is a collapsed alternative, which I like in principle, but I did not see what it contained.
- Watercolor: the pick is a 48-color set with 3 brushes and a case, yet the page also says "Then add: Transon 8-piece brush set" and the kit list says "student pan set ... two brushes" and the first session says "three colors only". 48 colors is more than the advice calls for and the brush add-on is redundant with the box's brushes. Mild contradiction.

## Task 4: six or more product cards across the shop
Looked at: A5 hardcover journal; Scotch glue sticks; floral washi 21 rolls; cottagecore ephemera pack; Pigma Micron pens; 48-color Artecho watercolor set; Transon 8 brushes; heavyweight watercolor pad; UHAPEER air-dry clay kit; Shuttle Art acrylics 15 tubes; Mod Podge gloss spray; mahjong sets (blue and white); Bradove embroidery kit.
- Would want: the air-dry clay kit (everything in one box, honest line about drying 24-48 hours per listing); the watercolor pad (140 lb cold-press for practice washes, the right thing); Pigma Micron pens (known, reliable); Scotch glue sticks (plain and sensible); the embroidery learning kit.
- Generic or odd: the 200-piece "cottagecore" ephemera pack and 21-roll floral washi (volume-pitch, aesthetic cliche); "Mod Podge gloss sealer spray" described only as a gloss spray, no mention that it can be tacky or yellow; Mahjong sets in "Three good first buys" are odd next to a journal and clay kit on a page whose headline is "Start the hobby. Buy one thing." Two nearly identical mahjong sets (blue and white tiles, different brands) appear on the same page, the second under "Gifting", which reads like padding. Several brands are unknown marketplace names (Artecho, Hapikalor, Knaid, UHAPEER, Stationery Island, TIASUSAN); the site says it checked the listings, but with no ratings or my own ability to see them, trust relies on the site.
- Card copy is clean and consistent but samey ("X in one box", "Good for gifting: it's all in one box" repeats on three cards). A small "Good for gifting" line on a clay kit is fine; on every boxed item it feels templated.

## Task 5: product pictures - PARTLY (main gap)
- There are no product photos. Every product card uses a coloured placeholder tile with a small icon and a tagline ("Paint right away", "A page to fill", "Brush in hand"). I could not see what the journal, clay kit or paints look like, which for a visual buyer of crafts is the main hesitation.
- The real photography is lifestyle imagery (hands on watercolor, a pottery table, a junk-journal spread, a picnic), and it is lovely, but pages label it "illustrative styling, not specific products". The methodology says these were made with AI tools. So the most appealing images are AI and not what I'd receive.
- Repeats: the same pink watercolor/embroidery flat-lay is used for "The Creative Girl" and "The Journaling Era" and for the "Are Craft Starter Kits Worth It?" article card; the same book-nook/stack of books image is used for "The Bookish Girl" and "Cozy After Hours"; the junk-journal spread photo appears on the Creative Corner hero and the Junk Journaling card; the same pottery photo for Pottery Era and Air-Dry Clay. Several hobbies show only a title tile (Crochet, Embroidery, Calligraphy, Paint by Numbers).
- Missing: real product shots (with credit), a finished-project photo by a human maker for each hobby, size/scale cues (what does "A5" look like, how big is "3 lb" of clay).

## Task 6: disclosure / methodology vs what I saw - DONE, matches
- /affiliate-disclosure and /methodology say: no prices, ratings or review counts; "We don't currently earn from these links"; "See it on Amazon" = named product page, "Find ... on Amazon" = search; products not tested by the site unless a page says so; photos are either credited product photos or plain labels; illustration images are AI-made styling. All of this matched what I saw: no prices anywhere, buttons and URLs match (dp pages vs s?k searches), placeholder tiles instead of product photos, "illustrative styling" captions on collection pages.
- Points: "We read each Amazon page ourselves before a product goes on a list" (shop header) plus "we have checked it against its Amazon listing" is a listing check, not testing, and the methodology is honest about it. But the shop headline "We read each Amazon page ourselves" can be read as more than that; I would soften it. "Last verified dates" are described, but I did not see a visible "last checked" date on the shop cards. The disclosure is reachable from every product block (small grey text, easy to miss but present). Honest note: it says "we don't currently earn", which is unusual; I believe it because the page also says the note will change if links go live, but it also makes me ask why the site exists (business model unclear).
- The "Editors" byline and an honest "we have not tested" are consistent, but the About/credibility of who actually did the reading is thin.

## Mobile 390x844 (brief)
- Home, Junk Journaling hobby page and Creative Girl shop page: no horizontal scroll (scrollWidth 390). Layout stacks cleanly, sticky header OK.
- Pages are very long: the junk-journaling hobby page is about 11,150px, the Creative Girl shop page about 13,800px. The one-pick section is far down; "See what you need" jump link helps. I did not test touch menu or list saving in depth.

## What delighted me
- "Start with what you have", and the line "note the one thing you truly wished you had. That is your first purchase."
- Honesty: per-listing language ("per the listing"), no fake ratings, no prices to go stale, explicit "not tested".
- One clear pick per hobby, extras marked "later". Low-cost framing ("$0 to $20").
- Warm, consistent visual identity (cream, brick red, script accents), friendly 404.
- Practical beginner content: three-colors rule, tearing instead of cutting, "my pages look messy" answers.

## What made me hesitate or leave
- No product pictures: I can't picture or judge the thing I'm asked to click on.
- Hero and lifestyle photos are AI-made and reused across cards; some hobby tiles have no photo.
- No named author, no human maker voice or finished pieces; "Editors" and same-day dates feel mass-produced.
- Unknown brands, no prices, no ratings: I must leave the site to learn anything about cost or reviews.
- Watercolor pick (48 colours, brushes included) vs advice (three colors, two brushes, then add 8 brushes) is inconsistent; mahjong sets and ephemera pack feel like filler.
- Some guides link only to Amazon searches ("Find ... on Amazon") while the hobby page names a product.

## Top 8 fixes ranked by impact
1. Add real, credited product photos to every named-product card (or at least a licensed/brand image), replacing icon placeholder tiles.
2. Add real human proof: a named author or maker, a photo of a real finished junk-journal page, clay dish, painting, per hobby; disclose AI imagery next to the image, not only on the methodology page.
3. De-duplicate imagery: give each era/hobby its own picture (Creative Girl vs Journaling Era, Bookish vs Cozy After Hours; fill Crochet/Embroidery/Calligraphy tiles).
4. Reconcile the watercolor kit: pick a smaller beginner set or drop "Then add brushes", and align with "three colors, two brushes" copy.
5. Trim filler picks: the 200-piece ephemera pack, duplicate mahjong sets, templated "good for gifting" lines; keep "buy one thing" true.
6. Give a rough price band on each product card (like "about $10-20") or say why none is shown, to cut the leave-the-site friction.
7. Make named links consistent between articles and hobby pages (use the same named picks, not searches), and expose "Prefer something different?" content or its count meaning.
8. Shorten mobile pages and surface the one-pick block sooner; show a visible "listing checked on [date]" on cards; soften "We read each Amazon page ourselves".
