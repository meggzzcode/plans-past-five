## 2. Escrow
- Escrow.com's own domains page: 2.6% with a $50 minimum for $0 to $5,000; 5.2% concierge (https://www.escrow.com/domains). VERIFIED.
- Flippa's help page (buyer-paid): 3.25% with a $10 minimum for $0 to $5,000, with a 20% discount through Flippa (https://support.flippa.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000774276). VERIFIED. The two pages differ; the Escrow.com page looks newer. Confirm at checkout.
- Credit-card funding adds a processing fee (third-party reports 3.05%); not verified on an official page.
- FlippaPay: from 0.5% for sales of $10,000 and over, paid by the buyer. VERIFIED (Flippa help page).
- Buyer verification: card hold of $5 before bidding, plus $500 hold at $5,000 or more. VERIFIED (same page).

At $1,495 the Escrow.com fee is the $50 minimum, which is about 3.3% of the price.

## 4. Pricing recommendation
No evidence supports a specific price from comparable sales. The pack's sensitivity points ($995 to $2,950) cannot be tested against sold comps. A defensible approach given what I found:
- Anchor the price on cost to rebuild (domain, hosting, content, design, tooling), not on revenue (there is none).
- List near the middle of the range with a reserve you would accept; expect negotiation. Fees on a $1,495 deal are small (about 12%: listing, 10%, escrow minimum), so fees are not the deciding factor.
- Treat the asset as a starter site: a buyer pays for time saved, not earnings. Do not use revenue multiples.

I cannot give an evidence-backed asking price or negotiation range. Record that limit in the listing notes.
