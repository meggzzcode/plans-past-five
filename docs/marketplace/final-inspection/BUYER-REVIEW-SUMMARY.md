# Buyer review summary (all rounds)

> **EVERY SCORE AND EVERY MONEY STATEMENT IN THIS FILE IS SIMULATED AI SENTIMENT.** It comes from AI personas playing buyers. It is not buyer research, not an offer, not a valuation and not evidence that anyone would pay anything. No comparable sales were used or found. The seller has no revenue, traffic or audience to measure. Scores (1 to 10) are copied from report tables as written; means are plain arithmetic on the rows shown. Reviewer sets and builds differ between rounds, so changes between rounds are not proof of improvement.

Two questions are kept apart:
- **"Would I buy the site?"** is the buyer question (this file). It is about a code, content and brand bundle that is pre-revenue and not live.
- **"Would I buy the supplies?"** is a customer question (`CUSTOMER-REVIEW-SUMMARY.md`). The two are never averaged or compared.

## 4. The three buyer personas

"Hypothetical money statements" below are what each simulated persona said about itself, quoted as order-of-magnitude, not as a price, a valuation or a comparable sale. Running-cost figures are from `final-inspection/OPERATING-COSTS-VERIFIED.md` (minimum about $1 a month, comfortable about $17 a month; some prices are secondary sources).

### B1 First-time online business buyer (not technical)
- **Attractive:** warm coherent brand, polished site that loads, real content and thorough, candid docs; low running cost.
- **Receives:** a pre-revenue, not-live code repository with 27 published articles at the time of review, a 43-hobby directory, four tools, brand files and 88 Pinterest pin images. No domain, host, analytics, mailbox, affiliate account or traffic.
- **Technical knowledge needed:** high for the first deploy. Per `BUYER-TAKEOVER.md`: not feasible alone for products, affiliate settings and first deploy; about half a day for a developer. Articles can be edited in a browser only after the Pages CMS login is tested (not yet).
- **Ongoing effort:** seller docs say about 30 minutes a week plus writing; B1 judged this true only for someone who can run Bun, Git and build commands.
- **Risks transferred:** image rights, Amazon approval, Netlify free credit cap pausing the site, legal pages are starter text, 4 originals not source-checked at the time.
- **Work before monetization:** own domain and mailbox, host, Search Console, Amazon Associates (a new account), contact details, lawyer review.
- **Outside help:** developer or AI coding tool, possibly a lawyer.
- **Hypothetical commitment (persona statement):** only at a very low "starter" price and with hands-on help; said, as an order of magnitude, "low hundreds up to roughly $1,000" for files and a plan, not income. Willingness 3 of 10.

### B2 Experienced affiliate-site investor
- **Attractive:** clean technical SEO (titles, canonicals, JSON-LD, sitemap), original client-side tools, $1 to $17 a month cost, honest docs and a 20-step transfer plan.
- **Receives:** the same bundle; no indexed pages, backlinks, keyword or SERP data, domain history or Search Console.
- **Technical knowledge needed:** moderate to high; stack is TanStack Start, React 19, Bun and server-rendered hosting, not WordPress.
- **Ongoing effort:** writing and link checking are manual; maintenance 5 of 10 for this persona.
- **Risks transferred:** Amazon Associates account is not transferable and nobody applied, so the buyer carries the 180-day, 3-sale qualification; AI-drafted content and AI images; scaled-AI content risk on a new domain; Netlify cap.
- **Work before monetization:** apply to Associates, publish, get indexed, build traffic; no second network is verified.
- **Outside help:** SEO and content support; developer for changes beyond articles.
- **Hypothetical commitment (persona statement):** "low hundreds to roughly $1,000 to $1,500" as the asset stood; said the seller's earlier higher anchor was above what it would pay without a live, indexed site. Said willingness would rise to 5 to 6 with a live indexed domain and 8 or more weeks of Search Console data. Willingness 3 of 10.

### B3 Lifestyle content creator acquiring a brand
- **Attractive:** brand name and tone, palette and type, 12 logos, 11 board covers, 7 pin templates, 88 pins with a 90-day schedule, original tools.
- **Receives:** brand kit, pin library, codebase, drafts; no audience, account or human voice.
- **Technical knowledge needed:** moderate; wants to edit without a developer, but hobbies, products and layout need code.
- **Ongoing effort:** creator must supply the personal layer (face, voice, photos) because the site has no named author and uses AI images.
- **Risks transferred:** unverified image rights (scored 3, lowest), unconfirmed domain and no trademark clearance (knockout only), starter-text legal pages.
- **Work before monetization:** replace or license images, add a human author, prove CMS, apply for affiliate accounts, build distribution.
- **Outside help:** developer or AI tool, photographer, lawyer for images and trademark.
- **Hypothetical commitment (persona statement):** "low hundreds of dollars to low thousands", for brand kit and pins, not as a business. Willingness 4 of 10.

## 6. What the buyers said would make them more likely to offer

These are persona wishes, not requirements. Several are within the owner's control; items that depend on Cowork are marked.

1. A live domain the seller owns and pushes, with a few weeks of Search Console data (B2 said 8 or more weeks would raise its willingness from 3 to 5 or 6). Needs the release deploy, then time. Cannot be shortened.
2. A recorded real edit and deploy through the CMS (B1, B3, R3-5). Cowork plus owner logins.
3. Written image-terms position, or replaced and licensed images, plus real photography with permission for products (B1, B2, B3). Cowork reads terms; owner sends permission emails.
4. Domain registrar proof and a trademark check (B1, B2, B3).
5. Keyword and SERP evidence per article, and a verified second affiliate program or display option (B2).
6. A named human editor and some first-hand testing notes, if the buyer is a creator (B3, R3-5).
7. A working contact address and a filled publisher block (all).
8. One verified count set and one buyer index (B1, B2, B3). Largely done; re-run the inventory before listing.
9. A binding, reviewed support offer or a paid onboarding package (B1).
10. Evidence from real people: results from the usability script (`docs/marketplace/USABILITY-TEST-SCRIPT.md`) kept separate from these AI scores, and a blind re-review on the live URL (Cowork workstream K).

## 7. Honest limits of this summary

- Buyer reviews B1 to B3 ran on an earlier build. Several of their blockers (secrets scan, doc counts, importer safety, publish gate, analytics loader, takeover rehearsal) have since had work recorded. Whether those changes satisfy the personas has not been tested; no re-run exists.
- Willingness scores are low (3 to 4 out of 10 in the final inspection, 4 in R2 and R3 where scored) because the asset is pre-revenue and not live. That is a description of the asset, and is not a defect list.
- No score here predicts what a real buyer would do, or that a buyer exists at any price.
