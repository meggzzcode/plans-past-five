# R2-2 Creative Hobby Buyer review

NOTE: simulated reviewer opinion, not real customer data. Persona: 31-year-old, wants a creative hobby, will buy supplies, wary of affiliate blogs and AI filler. Tested desktop 1280x800 and briefly 390x844 against http://127.0.0.1:5950. No purchase made, no external link clicked.

## Task 1: pick a hobby. Outcome: done
Homepage (hero photo, "Find Your Thing", "Shop Your Hobby"), then Creative Corner. Found six creative hobbies with time, cost and energy tags (junk journaling, scrapbooking, watercolor, embroidery, crochet, knitting). Picked Junk Journaling ($0-20, 45 min, "start tonight"). About 3 clicks, under a minute. Hobby page is clear: "Why you might love it", 4-step start, "your first session", "what you'll need". Hero photo is lovely.

## Task 2: article. Outcome: done
"How to Start Junk Journaling: A Beginner's Guide" (3 min). It reads like someone who has done it: keep an envelope in your bag for café receipts, tear rather than cut, three-colour rule, pockets and tip-ins, "glue stick not liquid glue, close under something heavy". The 6-step first 30 minutes is concrete. The "Common worries" section is warm. It starts with what you already own and does not push buying; it links to the supplies guide in the second paragraph. Supplies feel natural. Hesitations: the byline is "The Plans Past Five Editors" and the editorial page says AI is used for research and content and that images are AI-made, so it is honest but not a person. Tone is slightly generic in places ("There's no wrong way to do it"). The related-article title "So, You Want to Start Junk Journaling? Here's Everything You Actually Need" is the most blog-formula title on the site, and the two junk journaling articles overlap. Small inconsistency: the article says 5 essentials, the hobby page 4, the supplies guide 6. The same flatlay photo appears as hero, inline and again in the related card, and the hobby page reuses it too.

## Task 3: supplies and the one thing to buy. Outcome: done, with reservations
Hobby page has "Shop your junk journaling kit" and "If you only buy one thing": Paperage blush blank journal, 160 pages, "View on Amazon" (amazon.com/dp/..., new tab, noopener). The article says the one place quality matters is thick pages, and the pick lists no paper weight; the "also consider" A5 journal says 120 gsm and "a better bet for layered pages". So the site's own pick is weaker than its alternative by its own logic, and I'd pick the alternative or a thrifted hardback. I'd click View on Amazon for the alternative to read the page, because the page says it is the product's own page, no prices are shown (I would not know cost until Amazon), and no commission is claimed ("we don't currently earn from these links"). That honesty makes me more willing to click. But I would hesitate that there is no price or photo here, so I'm trusting a pastel placeholder. Supplies guide also says "free first": check your house, make one page, buy the thing you wished you had. That is the most trustworthy content on the site.
Two different shopping lists on the same hobby page (3 "Browse options" cards, then a kit of named products) is slightly redundant; "Browse options" opens searches (e.g. amazon.com/s?k=washi+tape+set).

## Task 4: product cards reviewed (12)
Wanted: Pigma Micron 8-pack (known, solid), Scotch glue sticks (boring but right), Artecho 48-colour watercolour set (full kit, but 48 colours is a lot for a beginner), UHAPEER air-dry clay kit (everything in one box; the page also warns clay is not food-safe, which I appreciated), Transon brush set, A5 120 gsm journal.
Generic or odd: blush journal as the "one thing" (see above); Mod Podge gloss spray (Amazon spray in an indoor-craft kit, though ventilation note is good); Knaid "Cottagecore ephemera pack, 200 pieces" and 21-roll floral washi (feel like Amazon filler, off-brand, contradicts the article's "pick one lane / mixed set with plain colours" and "ephemera can wait"); Benicci 12 brushes; Hapikalor paper (unknown brand). Many brands are Amazon-marketplace names; there are no Arches-style brand heroes except as "step up" options. Bookish and Cozy collections have no named products at all, only "what to look for" search cards (the site says so openly); "Miniature book nook kit" is an odd item for a "start a hobby" site. The collection-level essentials lists blur several hobbies together (Creative Girl lists notebook, scrapbook album, printed photos, adhesive and watercolor pans under one heading), which is confusing.

## Task 5: product pictures. Outcome: missing
No product has a photo. Every product card shows a flat grey-blue box reading e.g. "JOURNALS / PAPERAGE". Collection pages say "illustrative styling, not specific products" and use AI-made lifestyle images, reused across collections (shop-pottery-era, shop-creative-girl and shop-bookish-girl images repeat on most shop pages). The methodology says cards use either a licensed product photo or a plain label, so this is disclosed, but as a buyer I cannot see colour, size, what is in the 200 pieces or the 48-pan case. That is the biggest hole for willingness to buy. Missing: real photos, size/colour, price band, one human photo of the maker's own finished pages, any "we made this page" proof.

## Task 6: disclosure vs what I saw. Outcome: mostly matches
Disclosure, methodology and editorial standards agree with each other and with the buttons: "View on Amazon" opens a named product page, "Browse options" opens a search, no prices or ratings shown, "We don't currently earn from these links", products not tested by the site, images AI-made, no fake experiences. All verified on the pages. Nuances: (a) "we checked its Amazon listing" is not the same as testing, and the buttons give no date I could see on cards (the methodology says dates are recorded); (b) "illustrative styling... made with AI tools" is stated on methodology but only a short label on collections; (c) the claim "We don't currently earn" is unusual and reassuring, yet makes the business model unclear; (d) the amazon links carry rel=noopener only, consistent with no affiliate tag, I did not click. No leaked internal notes or raw markdown seen.

## Mobile 390x844
No horizontal scroll on home, hobby, article, shop pages (scrollWidth 390). Hamburger nav, hero photo, kit header and product card stack cleanly; the placeholder box is large and eats a screen before the title and Amazon button. Readable, tidy.

## What delighted me
Honest "free first, buy only what you wished you had" framing; "No-spend swap" tips (thrifted hardback); the first 30 minutes steps; the "also consider" comparison lines; clean, warm visuals; no prices or fake star ratings; candid disclosure and the clay food-safety warning.

## What made me hesitate or leave
No product photos; placeholder cards; the one-thing pick contradicts the thick-paper advice; AI-made imagery and "Editors" byline; repeated images; overlapping junk journaling articles and inconsistent essential counts; some Amazon filler brands; empty-feeling Bookish/Cozy shop pages.

## Top 8 fixes
1. Add real product photos (licensed) or at least size/colour detail on cards.
2. Make the "one thing" journal pick match the article's thick-paper advice (state gsm; swap to the 120 gsm option).
3. Show a "checked on <date>" on each named card.
4. Replace or prune filler picks (200-piece ephemera, 21-roll floral washi) in line with "one lane, mixed set".
5. Reconcile essential counts and clean the collection-level essentials lists by hobby.
6. Merge or sharply differentiate the two junk journaling articles; rename the formula title.
7. Use real, varied photography; stop reusing the same flatlay and collection images.
8. Add a human voice or a "made by" example of a finished page.
