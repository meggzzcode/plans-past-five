# Review 2: Articles, Trust and Buying (reader persona)

**Read this first:** I am simulating ONE reasonable person (late 20s/early 30s, 9-to-5, tired by Tuesday, likes pretty things, suspicious of affiliate blogs and AI filler, limited time and money). This is one imagined reader, not data. Real tests with 5 real people (watch them use it on their own phone, say nothing, note where they stop) would be much better. Treat everything below as a hypothesis list.

Method: local build, 390x844 phone viewport, read innerText of 9 articles in full (junk journaling x2, mahjong, solo dates, picnic, pickleball, find-a-hobby, low-energy, 35 hobbies), the shop index, two collection pages (journaling-era, girls-night-in, plus a glance at sporty-girl), about, affiliate disclosure, editorial policy, methodology, corrections. Screenshots in /tmp/claude-0/review2/.

---

## Overall gut reaction

It looks lovely. The palette, type, and the photos make me want to stay. The headline writing is good ("Everything You Need for Your Pickleball Era", "Professionally off duty"). Then I start reading and it slowly turns into the same article wearing different hats: every piece opens with a hypothetical scene, then a sorted list, then the same four-box ritual (essentials / nice to have / save-splurge / "Your first 30 minutes" / "Try it this weekend" / "Make it a plan"). By article three I can predict the next block. By article five I suspect a template, and the honest "we use AI tools" line on the editorial page confirms it. The shopping is the weakest part: pretty site, but product cards are text-only, have no prices, and the explanations undercut themselves.

## Article-by-article

### 1. /journal/how-to-start-junk-journaling (about 2,300 words, "4 MIN READ")
- **Opening:** Best of the batch. "You know that pile of things you can't quite throw away? The ticket from the concert, the pretty café receipt, the tag from a dress you love..." That is a recognisable thing, not filler. I kept reading.
- **Length/skim:** Fine until "Ideas for your next ten pages" and "Common beginner worries"; I skimmed there. "4 min read" is optimistic for 2,300 words (that is closer to 9-10 min).
- **Structure:** Good on a phone. Short bullets, numbered steps, the pull-quote breaks it up.
- **Tips:** Concrete. "Pick two main colors from your main piece and one accent. A page in cream, red and green looks curated even if everything on it came from your recycling bin." "Tear slowly towards yourself for a nice feathered edge." I would try this tonight.
- **Voice:** Mostly friend-like. Canned bits: "Being good at it is completely optional." (pull-quote), "There are no rules, no wrong answers, and no reason for it to be neat. That's the whole appeal." and "Very main-character." / "MAKE IT A GIRLS' NIGHT" (reads like a brand trying to sound like us; also "girls' night" excludes people).
- **Image:** Hero (journal spread, cherry mug, washi, pens) matches and is genuinely pretty.
- **Callouts:** No-spend alternative ("Raid your recycling. Cereal boxes become sturdy backgrounds...") is the best callout on the site: specific and useful. Save/Splurge is thin ("A kraft notebook from the stationery aisle and one multipack of washi tape.") with no amounts.
- **Trust:** No firsthand claims, which is honest but means no "I tried this and X happened". No sources for anything.
- **Would I do it?** Yes. Highest "I'd do this tonight" of the set.

### 2. /journal/junk-journaling-supplies-for-beginners (about 2,500 words, "5 MIN READ")
- **Opening:** "Picture a craft aisle on a slow Sunday... Forty minutes later you are holding a basket and have no idea what any of it is for." Relatable and well turned, but "Picture..." is the third time I have been told to picture something.
- **It is a near-duplicate** of article 1. Same essentials list, same "what you'll need" box, same no-spend idea, same "first 30 minutes" steps, same "Everything looks chaotic" tip. Two sibling articles with the same checklist makes me trust neither as the "real" one.
- **Best content:** "Read acid-free and archival labels with care. They are marketing terms with no single official standard... look for adhesives that say they passed the Photographic Activity Test (ISO 18916)." That is the first line anywhere that sounds like a person who checked, and it is great. More of this.
- **Bug that hurts trust:** the "Make it a plan" box shows raw markdown on the page: "Put a journal evening on the calendar in [Make Some Plans](/make-plans)." The link is not a link. Looks unfinished.
- **Contradiction:** body says "These are product types, not specific products. Shop for the description and ignore the packaging." Then the shop block below is six specific branded products. Which is it?
- **Callouts:** The "Realistic shopping order" ("About nothing / A small basket / A bigger basket") is the right idea but gives me no dollar figures. "Prices vary by shop, so compare before you buy" is where I need a number.
- **Would I do it?** I would do the free version. I would not buy from the list as shown (see Shopping).

### 3. /journal/how-to-host-a-mahjong-night (about 2,700 words, "7 MIN READ")
- **Opening:** "Picture this. It's Friday, the group chat has been circling 'we should do something' for two weeks, and the plan on the table is the same loud restaurant where you shout across the table and leave hoarse." Good, specific. Then "Now picture the alternative" - same device twice in two paragraphs.
- **Strongest honest content on the site:** "First, 'mahjong' means several games... the set has to match the version you're playing." This saves me from buying the wrong set. Genuinely helpful, and the "Playing with respect" section is thoughtful.
- **Length:** Too long. "Common mistakes", "Troubleshooting", and "Making it a recurring tradition" repeat each other (pick a version, keep it short, agree rules). I quit around "Troubleshooting".
- **Wrong label:** The timeline starts "A week before: confirm the version..." but the box is titled "YOUR FIRST 30 MINUTES". It is a week-long plan, not 30 minutes. Reads like a template stamped on regardless.
- **Voice:** "tradition-in-waiting", "the sort of standing plan that ends up being the thing everyone protects on the calendar" and "Being good at it is completely optional; knowing which version you're playing is not." (second site-wide use of that phrase).
- **Do I trust the claims?** Mostly yes, since it hedges. But "a big modern revival among women's social clubs" (on the shop/hobby card) has no source.
- **Image:** Beautiful pink gingham table with tiles, cookies and friendship bracelets. Matches the "girls' night" idea, though it is more a mood than a mahjong guide.
- **Would I do it?** Yes, but I would first need to find a friend who plays. The "Try it this weekend" message to the group chat ("Does anyone play mahjong, and which version?") is a smart, doable first step.

### 4. /journal/solo-date-ideas (about 3,600 words, "8 MIN READ")
- **Opening:** "Imagine you've got a free Thursday evening and nobody to fill it with. The old you would scroll until it was bedtime. The new you has an hour, a notebook and a very good idea about where to sit." "Old you / new you" is a generic self-improvement trope. Fine, not memorable.
- **Length:** Way too long on a phone: 50 items and about 30,000px of scrolling. I gave up at item ~25. Cut to 25 and add a "pick one for tonight" shortcut at the top.
- **Repetition (the clearest AI tell):** The same canned block repeats under multiple ideas: "You'll need: A library card or one book. first try → Pick up a short novel and read before your phone gets a chance. Read the reading guide" appears on items 1, 8, 11, 16, 38 and 41 (six times). "Museum" twice. Picnic twice. It makes the list feel padded and auto-generated.
- **Mismatched ideas:** Item 8 "Bookstore browse, no buying" gets "You'll need: A library card or one book" and "Read the reading guide". Item 43 "Cook something slightly ambitious" gets "first try → Bake one batch of cookies and give half away." Item 39 "Practice-wall tennis" alone says "first try → Book a free public court and just rally with a friend". It is a solo date! These cards are attached by category, not by thought.
- **Good bits:** "A note on feeling safe" is sensible; "Two personal tips, not official advice" is the right kind of honesty. "Eating alone is the part people dread most, and it's mostly a logistics problem" is real insight.
- **Tone:** "Dessert-only dinner: Skip the main course and go straight to the good part." I like that one. "Plan the next one" is a cute closer.
- **Would I do it?** Yes, one: coffee shop and a chapter, or the solo picnic. But I would not read all 50.

### 5. /journal/how-to-plan-a-perfect-picnic (about 1,800 words, "5 MIN READ")
- **Opening:** "Imagine the picture you have in your head: a big soft blanket, a wicker-ish basket... Now imagine the real version: a damp sandwich, a blanket that's too small, no way to open the bottle, and a patch of grass picked in a panic." That is the best opening of the batch. Funny and true.
- **Content:** Practical (timeline, spot, menu that travels). The food-safety paragraph ("keep cold food at or below 40 °F (4 °C)... one hour when it's above 90 °F") is useful, but it is a dense wall of text and sits oddly between "menu" and "make it look the part".
- **Voice slips:** Mixes American and British: "rubbish bag", "flapjacks", "bin anything", "wet wipes" next to "°F", "toilet" (the rest of the site says "restroom"-style American English: "cooler", "park"). Reads as unedited.
- **Copy-paste leak in shop block:** "Insulated water bottle: Keeps water cold for the whole match or trail." A picnic has no "match". That line is from the pickleball article (and appears there too, where it fits).
- **Timing:** It is November 19 and this is the Weekend Edit; no seasonal framing at all. Neither it nor the date line tells me this is a spring/summer plan. (Minor.)
- **"Your first 30 minutes"** works here (it is genuinely a picnic setup); good.
- **Would I do it?** Yes in good weather. The "Keep it tiny: one friend, two sandwiches and a thermos, ninety minutes" prompt is perfect.

### 6. /journal/pickleball-for-beginners (about 1,500 words, "4 MIN READ")
- **Opening:** "Imagine a friend sends a message on a Thursday: 'Four of us are playing pickleball Saturday. You're coming.' It isn't a question. You've heard the name a hundred times and still couldn't say whether the ball is hit with a racket, a paddle or a very small tennis shoe." Funniest line on the site. "Very small tennis shoe" is the one phrase that sounded like an actual person.
- **Tips:** Concrete (court dimensions, "kitchen", double-bounce, how to join open play: "Hi, I'm new. Can I join the next game?"). That last line is the most doable sentence I read all day.
- **Trust:** "per the official rulebook, approved balls are fine indoors or outdoors" - sounds sourced but gives no link or name. I cannot verify it.
- **Image mismatch:** The hero has a tennis racket, tennis balls and a pickleball paddle with a pickleball. A newcomer could get confused about which is which in an article that is literally explaining the difference.
- **Shop block:** Includes "Beginner tennis racket" in a pickleball article. Why would I buy a tennis racket for pickleball? It says so itself earlier: "not a stringed racket". Contradiction.
- **Make-it-a-plan CTA:** "Sorry, I have plans past five." Charming; first time the brand voice has a real joke.
- **Would I do it?** Yes, probably the most likely "text a friend right now" article.

### 7. /journal/hobbies-to-try-when-life-feels-like-work-sleep-repeat (about 3,600 words, "6 MIN READ")
- **Opening:** "You know the loop. Alarm, emails, meetings, the 3 p.m. slump, one more email, closing the laptop at six and somehow ending up on the couch watching someone else's life on your phone until midnight." Spot on for the target reader. Real hook.
- **Canned:** "Being good at it is completely optional." (third site use - also in junk-journaling and mahjong and find-a-hobby; it is now clearly a template line). "Your free time doesn't need a performance review." is a good pull-quote.
- **Length:** 35 hobbies, each with the same time / cost / solo / "You'll need" / "first try" block. Skimmable but numb-making; sorted by energy is a smart idea.
- **Same blocks as article 4:** "You'll need: A library card or one book." "first try → Pick up a short novel..." again. I have now seen it eight times across articles.
- **Would I do it?** I'd pick paint-by-numbers or a puzzle. But I would not remember it as a distinct article from "20 Cozy Hobbies".

### 8. /journal/low-energy-hobbies (about 2,400 words, "5 MIN READ")
- **Opening:** "Picture it: you get home at six-fifteen, put your bag down in a spot that will annoy you tomorrow, and stand in the kitchen eating crackers over the sink." Best "tired Tuesday" line anywhere. I felt seen.

## Cross-article findings

### Voice: friend or AI?
- Friend-like: the pickleball opener, the picnic opener, "Sorry, I have plans past five.", "Very main-character.", "Future you will be smug."
- AI-like / template: every article has the same skeleton; "Being good at it is completely optional" x4; "Keep it short, warm and clearly labeled as practice"; "a tradition-in-waiting"; "It's less a party than..."; "Nothing is wrong, exactly."; "the whole appeal"; "which is a fine place to start"; "That's the whole method". Phrases like "the gentlest first solo date there is" (You Might Also Love card) sound like a card on a product box.
- **Opening device overuse:** Of the 9 I read, 7 begin with "Picture..." / "Imagine..." (supplies: "Picture a craft aisle"; mahjong: "Picture this."; solo: "Imagine you've got"; picnic: "Imagine the picture"; pickleball: "Imagine a friend sends"; find-a-hobby: "Imagine someone asks"; low-energy: "Picture it:"). By the fourth, that is the single most obvious AI tell. The first article (junk journaling) and 35-hobbies ("You know the loop") open without it, and they are the stronger ones. Vary or drop it.
- The site says all bylines are "THE PLANS PAST FIVE EDITORS". No face, no name, no first person. I understand the honesty reason (it does not fabricate experience), but as a reader I read it as "nobody wrote this".

### Length
Long for a Tuesday-night phone. Word counts: solo dates 3,600; 35 hobbies 3,600; mahjong 2,700; supplies 2,500; low-energy 2,400; junk journaling 2,300. The "N MIN READ" labels understate them (2,300 words labelled "4 MIN READ"). That feels like a small lie, and I notice it. Cut the end sections ("Common mistakes", "Troubleshooting", "Ideas for the next ten pages") by half.

### Structure/scannability
Good bones: headings, short paragraphs, numbered steps, "In this story" collapsible. The pull-quotes are pretty. Problem: repeated boxed blocks make the page feel like a form. On one article I count: essentials box, nice-to-have box, no-spend callout, save/splurge, first-30-min, try-it-this-weekend, make-it-a-plan, shop block, "if you loved this" cards, "you might also love" cards. That is ten boxes. Fewer would feel more human.

### Callout blocks (do they help or clutter?)
- **No-spend alternative:** helps, because it is specific. Keep.
- **Save / Splurge:** vague and un-priced ("A properly insulated bag and a roomy waterproof-backed blanket"). It does not tell me how much. Add numbers or cut.
- **Your first 30 minutes:** helps on picnic, low-energy, junk journaling. Wrong/misleading on mahjong, solo date, find-a-hobby.
- **Make it a plan:** feels like a generic ad-slot for the site's own tool (the same "Find Your Thing / Let's Make Plans" buttons at the bottom of every article). Good on pickleball because of the joke; elsewhere clutter. The raw-markdown bug on the supplies page kills it.
- **Try it this weekend:** best single-action CTA. Keep and move higher.
- **"If you loved this, try..." + "You might also love":** On the junk-journal page the same Scrapbooking / Collage / Junk Journaling cards appear twice (once as "If you loved this", then again after "You might also love"). Visible duplication.

### Images
Very pretty, consistent colour palette, clearly the site's best asset. They look AI-generated (the editorial page admits "The images on this site were created with AI tools and depict no real people or brands"), which is honest but means the picnic hero women and the mahjong table are not real photos. The pickleball hero mixing a tennis racket with the paddle is the one real mismatch. The shop hero says "illustrative styling, not specific products" - good that it says so, but it also means no collection has a real product photo.

### Trust: claims, sources, disclosures
- **No sources list on any article.** Phrases like "per the official rulebook", "park services advise against hiking alone", "ISO 18916", "Hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol" are good but unlinked. The methodology page mentions "Last verified dates... When a story's facts have been checked against sources, we record that date." I saw no verified date on any article, only a publish date. It promises something I cannot find.
- **No fake firsthand claims.** I looked for "I tried / we tested / our editors loved" and did not find any. That is good and unusual for this kind of site. The about page says "We don't fabricate personal experiences, product tests, reviews or community numbers." I believe it because the articles also never say it.
- **Defensiveness.** The "What we won't do" list on /editorial-policy reads like a defensive checklist ("Invent personal experiences... Publish fake reviews... Invent local businesses"). A brand-new reader doesn't think of those things; listing them makes me think about them. Short and positive would feel more confident.
- **AI disclosure** ("We use AI tools to help with research, content development, organization and website operations... The images on this site were created with AI tools") is honest and I respect it, but it is buried on one page and it admits what I already suspected from the repetition. It builds trust for me because it is blunt; it also confirms that no human tested anything.
- **Publication dates:** Nearly all articles are dated November 18-19, 2026 (the same two days), with "Details checked 2026-11-18" on products. A brand-new site with 100+ articles all dated this week looks like a content dump. Back-dating would be dishonest; but I notice it.
- **Corrections page:** "Our inbox is being set up. Once it is live, it will be listed on the contact page." and "No corrections yet." A corrections policy with no way to submit corrections reduces trust. Either get an email address up or remove the promise.
- **Pages contradict each other (this is the biggest trust issue):**
  - /shop and /affiliate-disclosure: "We recommend product types... rather than pushing one brand" / "We don't list prices, star ratings or reviews" / "Buttons say exactly where they go: 'Browse options on Amazon' means a search page".
  - /methodology: "We do not show prices, star ratings, brands or other details we have not verified".
  - But the junk-journaling and mahjong articles and the two collection pages show specific brands (Scotch, Sakura Pigma Micron, Mr. Pen, Knaid, TIASUSAN, MUTEX, National Mah Jongg League) and full listing titles with "View on Amazon" buttons and "Details checked 2026-11-18".
  - And the disclosure text differs: product-card blocks say "Links go to ordinary Amazon product pages. We don't currently earn from them", the other blocks say "Links open an Amazon search".
  So a careful skeptic (me) finds the site's own pages saying opposite things. That reads as sloppy at best, and makes me wonder what else is not true.

## SHOPPING

### What I looked at
/shop (index), /shop/journaling-era, /shop/girls-night-in (plus /shop/sporty-girl skim), and the "Shop the hobby" block inside the junk-journal supplies article and the mahjong article.

### Shop index (/shop)
- Pretty collection tiles ("The Pottery Era", "The Creative Girl", "The Sporty Girl", "Girls' Night In") with good photos. Names are fun but I can't tell what is in each one without clicking ("The Creative Girl: Paper, paint and thread for quiet evenings").
- Tagline: "36 product types that make starting a new hobby a little easier... We describe what to look for rather than pushing one brand, and we'll always tell you when you don't need to buy anything at all." Reassuring, not salesy. Good. The closing line "There are no prices, ratings or brand claims, because those change and we haven't tested them." is honest but, as a shopper, it tells me: no help deciding. And it is wrong on the collection pages where brands appear.
- "The Sporty Girl" / "The Creative Girl" / "The Bookish Girl": slightly dated "-girl" labelling; "Pottery Era / Journaling Era" works better. Pick one logic.

### Collection pages
- **Journaling era (/shop/journaling-era):** Starts with five generic "What to look for" cards (Junk journal, Washi tape, Stickers, Reading journal, Pocket notebook) with "look for → Kraft or mixed-paper pages and a lay-flat binding." Then a "Starter kit" with real branded products. Two different shopping systems stacked on one page, and I do not know which to use. "What these hobbies involve" and "Essentials / Optional upgrades" lists include scrapbook album, printed photos, corner punch, frame, book light: a jumble that mixes three hobbies.
- "Essentials: ✶ Scrapbook album ✶ Printed photos ✶ Adhesive ✶ Old magazines" - those are not essentials for junk journaling. This blurs "essential vs optional" again.
- **Girls' night in (/shop/girls-night-in):** Essentials list: "One group-friendly craft, Snacks, A table, A mahjong set (or a borrowed one), Three friends, A rules card, Bead kit with elastic cord, Beading kit with wire, clasps and beads" - "Three friends" as a shopping essential made me smile, but it is also what an auto-merged list looks like (mahjong + bracelets + jewelry making all merged).
- No collection page shows a single product photo.

### The product cards (what makes me want to buy?)
Example, from /journal/junk-journaling-supplies-for-beginners:
> "01 ESSENTIAL / Available on Amazon / Blank Journal Notebook, 160 Pages, Medium 5.6 x 8 in (Blush) / PAPERAGE / The listing names junk journaling as a use and describes thicker acid-free paper than ordinary notebooks. / best for → Beginners who want a sturdy first journal / Worth knowing: 100 gsm paper will warp if the pages get glue-heavy; the listing does not promise it handles wet glue"

My honest reaction:
- **No photo.** I am being asked to buy a "Blush" notebook from a site whose entire pitch is aesthetics, and I cannot see it. Biggest single blocker. A pretty-things reader will not click out blind.
- **No price, not even a range.** I know why (it is stated policy), but I am budget-limited. "About $8" or "$10-15" would let me decide before I click. Right now I must leave the site to learn whether this is $6 or $26. The disclosure's reasoning "an out-of-date figure is worse than none" does not convince me; a range with "checked Nov 2026" would.
- **The "reason" often argues me out of it.** The first essential tells me its paper "will warp if the pages get glue-heavy; the listing does not promise it handles wet glue" - in an article about gluing things in. That is the item flagged ESSENTIAL. So is the notebook essential or not? Also "Black only" for the optional Pigma pens and "One width only (10 mm)" are honest, but a "Worth knowing" that undercuts every card makes me think "so why recommend it?".
- **Uneven language:** "A cheap paper adhesive from a familiar stationery brand" is a real reason. "Details checked 2026-11-18" is a nice honest detail but I do not know what "checked" means (price? stock? listing text?).
- **Unknown brands:** PAPERAGE, Knaid, Secopad, Stationery Island, TIASUSAN, Jongyance, MUTEX, GUSTARIA, Plusvivo. I only know Scotch, Sakura Pigma Micron and National Mah Jongg League. With no photos or reviews, unknown brand + no price = I will not click.
- **Too many options:** The junk-journal block gives 3 essentials + 2 optional + 6 "Another option for this slot" (11 cards). Four of those "alternatives" are more washi tape and tape runners. It says "Choose one, not all." but that is a decision job handed back to me, the tired person. Three "Another option" with no comparison is clutter. A "Pick this one if..." table or just one recommended + one budget would be better.
- **Essential vs optional:** the ESSENTIAL / OPTIONAL labels are clear, and I like them. But they do not match the article: the article says the essentials are "a glue stick, scissors, a pen, scraps" and a notebook you already own, yet the "Start with these" list is a $?? notebook, 4-pack glue sticks, 21-roll washi set. The article's own "washi tape is a nice-to-have" but the product block calls it ESSENTIAL. I notice that mismatch.
- **"Third-party seller: check the listing" notes (mahjong):** "Sold by a third-party seller on Amazon: check the listing" and "Worth knowing: Sold by a third-party seller; confirm seller and stock" on 4 of 6 mahjong products. It makes me hesitate, and the wording is a disclaimer, not information. What would help: why it matters ("returns and delivery come from the seller, not Amazon") and what to look for (return window, seller rating). As written, the site is passing its homework to me.
- **"Worth knowing: Edition is 2026: confirm when the next card is released before recommending a purchase"** (on the League card): this is an internal editor note leaking onto a reader-facing card ("before recommending"?). It is also awkward given the card is dated 2026 and it is November 2026. Needs to be rewritten for readers: "The 2026 card is the current one; a new card usually comes out later in the year".
- **"I need this / I already own this / Save for later" checkboxes:** Nice idea for a shopping list (a tired person can track), but it adds three controls per product and visually triples the card height. On a phone there are 11 cards x 3 controls. And "Save for later" is next to the same bookmark icon used for "Save to My Plans". Confusing.

### Does it feel like an affiliate blog?
Honestly, no, and I appreciate that. The site says "We don't currently earn from these links." It is the least pushy shop I have seen. But two things confuse me:
1. **Do you earn or not?** "We don't currently earn from these links" + the methodology "Some links may earn us a commission once affiliate programs are active". So the plan is to earn later. The wording is honest, but "currently" tells me the links will turn into affiliate links silently after I have trusted them. The disclosure promises to update "before any commission links go live" - fair, but I won't be checking. A line like "If this changes, links will say so beside the button" would help (it nearly does).
2. **Where do the links go?** Block 1: "Links go to ordinary Amazon product pages." Block 2: "Links open an Amazon search so you can compare options." Button text "View on Amazon" vs "Browse options on Amazon" is the real clue, but I only learned that from the disclosure page. The icon (a little arrow) is the same on both.

### Would I click through?
- **Generic "Browse options on Amazon" blocks (picnic blanket, pocket notebook, water bottle):** no. They are just a search link; I can type that into Amazon myself. These add nothing except a faint feeling of "affiliate blog" (even though it earns nothing). "Pocket notebook: For lists, sketches, bird logs and lines you want to remember." appears in four different articles (picnic, solo date, find-a-hobby and journaling era) with identical text. That repetition is my cue that these are filler.
- **Specific product cards (washi tape, Scotch glue sticks, mahjong set):** only the glue sticks and Sakura pens, because I know the brands. For the mahjong sets (a $50+ purchase with a lot of ways to get it wrong) I would click, but only after I found a price, and the page itself tells me to "confirm seller and stock".
- **Overall:** I would click on maybe 1 in 10 cards. The strongest candidate is the National Mah Jongg League card, because it is the official source and the article explained why I need it.

### What would make me more confident (shopping)
1. A real photo (or at least a thumbnail) of each specific product.
2. A price range or a "usually $X-Y" on every card, with "checked Nov 2026".

## Top 10 highest-impact fixes (ranked)

1. **Make the shopping policy and the shop match.** Decide: product types (search links) or specific listings, and make /shop, /affiliate-disclosure, /methodology, the card footers and button labels all say the same thing. Currently /methodology says "We do not show... brands" while cards show Scotch, Sakura, Knaid, etc. This is the most damaging trust problem because a skeptic can spot it in two clicks.
2. **Add a product image and a price range to every specific product card** ("about $8-12, checked Nov 2026"). Without a picture and a number, a pretty-things, budget-limited reader will not click, which makes the whole shop pointless.
3. **Add an "If you only buy one thing" pick and a starter budget at the top of each shop block, and collapse the rest.** Cut the 6 "Another option for this slot" cards to 1-2 with a one-line comparison. Reduce decision load for tired readers.
4. **Fix the visible bugs and leaks:** raw markdown "[Make Some Plans](/make-plans)" on /journal/junk-journaling-supplies-for-beginners; "Keeps water cold for the whole match or trail" on the picnic article; "confirm when the next card is released before recommending a purchase" on the mahjong card; "Your first 30 minutes" wrapping a week-long plan on mahjong and find-a-hobby; the tennis racket in a pickleball article. Each one says "no human read this".
5. **Break the "Imagine/Picture" opener habit** (7 of 9). Keep the best ones (pickleball, picnic, low-energy) and rewrite the others with a plain observation, a question, a number, or a first sentence in the second person without a hypothetical. Also retire "Being good at it is completely optional" (4 uses) and the repeated "Pocket notebook" / "Insulated water bottle" card copy.
6. **Cut length by about 30-40%** and make the "min read" honest. Solo dates to 25 ideas; merge "35 hobbies" and "20 cozy hobbies" or differentiate them clearly; merge the two junk-journaling articles (or make the supplies one only about buying, and stop repeating the essentials list). Move "Try it this weekend" up to the first screen.
7. **Fix the repeated mismatched idea cards** in lists (the "library card or one book / Read the reading guide" block under six different solo-date ideas; "cook something ambitious" -> cookies; "practice-wall tennis" -> "rally with a friend" on a solo list). Either write unique "first try" lines or drop the block where it does not fit.
8. **Add sources and a "last checked" date on each article** (a short "Sources" list: official rulebook, USDA/food-safety page, park-service advice, ISO 18916). The methodology promises "last verified dates" but I cannot find one. This is cheap and turns "sounds careful" into "is checkable".
9. **Make the corrections/contact real.** Add a working email on /contact and /corrections ("Our inbox is being set up" cancels the whole promise). Consider a named editor or a short "who we are" with at least a first name, because "The Plans Past Five Editors" + AI disclosure leaves me with no one to hold accountable.
