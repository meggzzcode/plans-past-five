# 01 Intent and research: Air-Dry Clay for Beginners (pilot, OFFLINE)

Prepared 2026-11-19. Offline pilot: nothing here is integrated into `content/` or `src/`. Slug stays `how-to-start-air-dry-clay` (URL preserved, per PLAN section 1).

## 1. Target reader
A woman, roughly 25 to 45, with a job that fills her head all day and an evening or a Saturday she would like to spend making something. She has probably seen air-dry clay trinket dishes on Pinterest or Instagram and assumes pottery means a wheel, a studio and a kiln. She wants a small, pretty, giftable object at the end, a low-stakes first try, and a clear list of what to buy (and what she already owns). She is not a child, a teacher or a sculptor. She will read on her phone while standing in a craft-shop aisle or at the kitchen table.

What she is anxious about: wasting money on the wrong clay, cracks, a result that looks "kid-made", mess on the table, and whether the finished piece is safe to use.

## 2. Search intent (live SERP read, 2026-11-19)
Main query searched: "air dry clay for beginners". Secondary: "air dry clay projects for beginners trinket dish jewelry holder", "air dry clay supplies needed beginners ...".
Method note: results came through a web search tool that returns titles, URLs and a summary. I did not open every result page; observations below are from titles, snippets and the pages marked "read". No search volumes are claimed because none were available.

What the top results do (observed):
- Main query: a mix of how-to guides ("A beginners guide to using air dry clay" potclays.co.uk; "Air Dry Clay for Beginners" artinhealthcare.org.uk; Skillshare class and blog; Gathering Beauty "10 tips and tricks"; ctpub.com; Art of Education "pros and cons"), plus brand roundups (Bob Vila "The Best Air-Dry Clays", a Yahoo Lifestyle roundup, Gathered "15 best air dry clay kits for 2026"). Content pattern: what it is, tools, techniques (pinch, coil, slab, scoring), drying, painting, sealing, tips, then a brand pick.
- Projects query: dominated by idea listicles and single-project tutorials (diyjoy, Hometalk, Mont Marte, Crayola jewelry dish, firstdayofhome, craftgossip). Trinket/ring dishes are the most repeated beginner project; pinch pots, magnets, ornaments and caterpillars recur.
- Supplies query: retailer and kit guides; short core list (clay, rolling pin, craft knife, sandpaper, acrylic paint, optional varnish).

Intent classification: primarily informational ("how do I start"), with a strong project/inspiration layer ("what can I make") and a latent commercial layer ("which clay / kit"). The reader wants all three in one place.

Gap we can occupy (judgement, from what the results look like): most pages are either a generic technique primer or a 10 to 30 idea list. Few appear to offer a small, ordered set of three projects with materials, drying and finishing per project and realistic timing, in a design-led voice. That is the format the owner chose. Keep the title clear: it must contain "air dry clay" and "beginners" or "projects".

Suggested primary keyword set (intent terms, not volumes): air dry clay for beginners; air dry clay projects; air dry clay trinket dish; air dry clay jewelry holder; what do you need for air dry clay.

## 3. The outcome the reader wants
By the end she can: pick one clay, know the three things worth owning besides it, make a dish she is proud to put on her dresser, understand that it needs days (not hours) to dry, finish it without a mishap, and know honestly what it cannot do (hold food, drinks or water; live outside).
Emotional outcome: a small object that is unmistakably hers, a slow evening with her hands busy, something to give a friend.

## 5. What to keep from the old article
Keep (technical and verified):
- No kiln or oven; dries on its own.
- Drying: 24 hours to touch, 72 hours full (Crayola); do not rush with heat; flip after a day (unsourced technique tip, low risk, hedged).
- Decorative only: not for food or drink even if sealed; sealing does not waterproof; display indoors.
- Seal only when fully dry, acrylic varnish, test first; follow spray-can ventilation instructions.
- Sanding when fully dry; mask optional, check SDS (do not claim required).
- Storage of unused clay: airtight, no added water.
- Troubleshooting content (cracks, rough edges, hardened clay).
- Substitutes the reader already owns (jar lid, butter knife, toothpick, baking paper, rolling pin or glass bottle).
- Internal links: how-to-host-a-craft-night, affordable-hobbies-under-25 and the junk-journaling article.
- The honest "decorative, not weatherproof" framing.
