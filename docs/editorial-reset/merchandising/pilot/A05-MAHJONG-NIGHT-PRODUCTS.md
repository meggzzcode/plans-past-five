Prepared 2026-11-19 on branch `editorial-reset`. Role: head of merchandising and retail buyer. Governed by PLAN.md sections 0000, 000, 00. Companion data: `A05-MAHJONG-NIGHT-PRODUCTS.csv` (every scored candidate and the full record for each selected product). Nothing here is approved, published or integrated; no importer run; no affiliate link created.

Method and honest limits. WebSearch plus page fetches on 2026-11-19. I read these maker or authority pages directly: National Mah Jongg League (League) store and home page, Yellow Mountain Imports (YMI) set pages, The Mahjong Line (TML) set, FAQ, rack and mat pages, Oh My Mahjong (OMM) set, kit, tile, mat, rack, card and napkin pages, Anders Ruff FAQ. Affiliate roundups saying "tested" publish no method and are treated as weak. No hands-on testing by us. Prices were read only to set bands and are not repeated for readers.

## 1. Who she is for this article (customer first)

Reuses M1 (research-labelled there; no new demographic claims here). She is hosting or starting a four-person American mahjong night, probably because a friend plays or a group invited her. M1 labels the mahjong revival among US women as a trend with NO participation figure (journalism; anecdotal). What follows are ASSUMPTIONS to test, informed by the product pages I read:

- Her fear is the wrong, incomplete or embarrassing purchase: wrong game (Chinese or Japanese set for American rules), no racks, missing jokers, no card. The maker and retailer pages I read repeat this: American play needs the current League card and a set with enough jokers (YMI, OMM, TML FAQ).
- She wants the table to look chosen, not like a games cupboard. Design-led brands exist because of that, and the Thingtesting staff piece (2026-09-14) says they often sell out and pre-order is "the norm". That is a supply risk we must plan around, not a quality signal.
- She does NOT need a pile. For one table of four she needs: one set (with four racks), four cards, a table that fits, and snacks that stay off the tiles. Everything else is optional.

## 2. What four players actually need (sourced facts)

| Need | What the sources say | Source (all accessed 2026-11-19) |
|---|---|---|
| Tiles | American play uses 152 tiles: 36 each of dots, bams, cracks; 16 winds; 12 dragons; 8 flowers; 8 jokers. Sets commonly add spares (YMI 166: adds 8 more flowers, 2 jokers, 4 blanks; OMM and TML 160: 10 jokers, 6 blanks). Spares are not part of standard play. | https://www.ymimports.com/pages/how-to-play-american-mahjong ; https://themahjongline.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions ; https://ohmymahjong.com/blogs/the-game-of-mahjong/the-beginners-guide-to-american-mahjong |
| Players | Always four (YMI); a "BOB" dummy can fill a fourth seat (OMM). | same |
| Racks and pushers | Four racks, one per player; pushers optional (YMI: "optional but highly recommended"). Many sets use all-in-one racks. | YMI guide |
| Case or bag | Varies: soft cases and totes; design-led sets list bags only. | maker pages |
| Card | Not in the set. Published annually by the League. Each player needs one under League-style play (corroborated only by a retailer press release; the League pages I read do not state it). | https://www.ymimports.com/pages/how-to-play-american-mahjong ; https://pr.norwoodtownnews.com/article/2026-National-Mah-Jongg-League-Card-Now-Available-at-The-Charleston-Club/6a0c75e22478c60002343979 |

| Tile compatibility | Tile sizes differ between makers. TML warns its tiles "may not fit racks, display cases or automatic tables made for other sizes". Listed tile dimensions: YMI 1.2 x 0.9 x 0.5 in; OMM 0.87 x 0.6 x 1.25 in; TML 1 x 1-7/16 x 7/16 in. | maker pages |

**Counting trap to avoid in copy.** Sources disagree on what a "166" set contains (6 or 4 blanks, 8 or 16 flowers, 8 or 10 jokers). We never state a tile count as a feature; the product card says "check the listing shows at least 8 jokers and 8 flowers".

- The card is paper, "not waterproof", keep from food and moisture (OMM retailer page). That supports our snack-table guidance; it does not justify a sleeve product (still rejected, M2 42 to 44).
- Year-proofing: the product record carries the year; article text says "the current year's card".

## 6. Visual merchandising concept: "The Mahjong Table" (Hosting Edit, placed per E2 near the top of the Host's Guide)

- **Layout.** One wide overhead table scene (styled, labelled as an illustration of the evening, never as product evidence), then a four-seat row of "place cards", one per seat, which is also the shopping unit. Seat 1: The Set. Seat 2: The Card. Seat 3 and 4 stay editorial: "The mat, if you want one" (criteria, no product) and "The side table" (snack and drink rules, no product). This makes the absence of extras look deliberate.
- **Grouping.** By what the table needs, not by product type. Two paths under The Set: "Ready to play tonight" (entry set) and "A table you are proud of" (design-led, with the rack caveat in the first line). Cards sit under both.
- **Card anatomy** (E3): name, one-line job, one-line reason, one-line limit, small "pairs with". No price, no stars, no scores, no "best".
- **Imagery.** Until rights are cleared the cards are text-only on a quiet tinted panel; hero uses an illustrated or own-photo table. Never a generated image standing in for a branded product.
- **Disclosure.** Amazon-affiliate cards carry the Associates-agreement wording once confirmed; editorial picks carry "not an affiliate link" so we never imply every link earns commission. Placement per E3 at the head of the block.
