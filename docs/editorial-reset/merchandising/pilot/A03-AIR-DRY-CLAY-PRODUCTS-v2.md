- Tools: web search and page fetches 2026-11-19. Customer forums were not reachable; customer voice is limited to search-summarised review text (anecdotal) and ThingTesting, Trustpilot and Amazon summaries seen second-hand. None of it is used for safety wording.

Seller type, variant, availability and affiliate eligibility are all unread. Repo notes from M2 ("sold by Amazon.com" for Galeria and DAS) are prior checks, not re-verified.
- Maker evidence was read for Mont Marte, Sculpd (help centre), Pott'd (EU blog), Crayola, DAS (fila.it), Liquitex and Winsor & Newton (v1 reads), Ateco, Sculpey, Posca (search summary) and Kemper (thin). Weakness in every case is stated.

## 6. Conflicts between makers that the writer must not smooth over

1. Thickness: 1/4 in (Crayola), 1 cm (Mont Marte tutorial), 1/2 in (Sculpd).
2. "Waterproof": Mont Marte FAQ and Sculpd listings versus Crayola and Pott'd ("does not make the clay fully waterproof") and Sculpd's own help (clay absorbs water).
3. Seal before or after painting: Mont Marte tips (2020) say before; its 2025 tutorial and Crayola say after. We follow the newer tutorial and Crayola.
4. Sealer drying between coats: maker FAQ "a few hours"; stockist and Amazon title text say about 30 minutes. Quote the maker; let her read the bottle.
5. Mont Marte names the product "Clay Varnish" in the FAQ and "Clay Sealer" on the product page.

## 8. Original product descriptions (from maker specs and this research only)

**Mont Marte Air Hardening Modelling Clay, white, 500 g.** This is the clay we would start a first ring dish with, because its maker publishes how long it takes to harden, what to avoid, and a trinket-dish method built around one 500 g block. It suits someone who wants a plain white ground for soft paint and would rather follow one maker's instructions from start to finish. You roll it, shape it, leave it to dry for the 24 to 48 hours the maker gives for its own tray, then paint. We chose it over DAS because DAS publishes much less online. Two limits: it is decorative only and not food safe, and the evidence is the maker's own, not an independent test.

**Winsor & Newton Galeria Acrylic, 20 x 12 ml.** A single box of ready-made soft shades, including blush, a butter yellow and an olive, so there is no mixing step on a Saturday morning. It suits someone who wants her three dishes to sit together on a dresser as a set. The maker names fine-art pigments and a safety certification statement for the colours. We picked it over a mix-your-own set because the palette is already close to the one in our magazine. It has more colours than three small pieces use, the tubes are small, and the maker does not mention clay, so the clay maker's advice to paint only once the piece is completely dry is the guide.

**Mont Marte Natural Finish Clay Sealer, 120 ml.** A brush-on, water-based finish that its maker says goes onto air-dry clay and keeps the natural look instead of adding shine. It suits someone who wants the quiet matte surface of a handmade dish and no spray fumes in the kitchen. The maker recommends two to three even coats applied with a soft, wide synthetic brush, and ventilation indoors. We chose it over gloss, solvent and spray options because none of those say they are for air-dry clay, and some are shiny or need more ventilation. A sealed piece is still decorative: it should not hold food, drink or water.

**DAS Air Hardening Modeling Clay, white, 1 kg.** A large white block from an Italian craft-clay maker that hardens in the air with no oven. It suits someone who wants one block for all three projects, plus a little to start again, and who may already know the name from a craft shop. The maker says it washes easily from hands and fabric. It is our alternative rather than our first choice because the maker's website gives no drying time or sealing advice, and a leaflet inside the pack is the only place that might. It is not for food or drink.

**Liquitex BASICS Acrylic Set, 12 x 22 ml.** Twelve tubes of artist-brand acrylic in primaries, earths, black and white. It suits someone who likes to mix her own blush, butter and matcha from white and does not mind a satin finish rather than matte. The maker documents lightfast pigments and a safety certification. We keep it as the alternative to the 20-tube set because it has no ready-made pastels, but its larger tubes will carry on into paint night and other projects. Check the box contents: the maker's own page counts the colours two ways.

## 10. Visual merchandising concept refresh: "The Clay Shelf, one palette"

- **Idea:** the shelf shows one coordinated palette, not three unrelated products. Row 1, **Start here: the clay, the colour, the finish** (numbered cards 1 to 3, one job each). Row 2, **Already in your kitchen** (quiet strip: rolling pin, butter knife, lid or template, parchment, sponge, fine sandpaper). Row 3, **When you want more**: one card for the 12-tube alternative; an optional pen card only if Posca clears Cowork; a gift-kit card only if the owner decides on Sculpd.
- **Palette strip:** a typographic row naming the three project tints (blush, butter, olive from the Galeria set) with a single cherry note; no product photograph is implied. Text-only until a licence clears.
- **Look (E3):** cream or natural-wood ground, one cherry note, at most two supporting tints, gingham as a prop only; styled still life labelled "inspiration" as the hero. Pieces shown dry and decorative, never holding food, drink or water.
- **Cards:** product, the job it does in the plan, one reason, one honest limit, price band as a small tag, no score, star or "tested". The disclosure line before the first link uses the Associates Agreement sentence.
- **Mobile:** cards stack at 16 px gutters in buying order.
- **Why she wants it:** one clear shelf, one palette, three finished dishes she can picture on her own dresser; and an honest list of what she already owns.
- **Words to avoid:** best, perfect, tested, favourite, must-have, non-toxic (for DAS), waterproof, food safe.
