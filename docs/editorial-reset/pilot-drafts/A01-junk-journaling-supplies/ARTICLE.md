## ARTICLE BODY

### Opening

The first page of a junk journal looks like it needs a craft-store trolley. It needs a thick book, something to stick things down with, one pen, and a roll or two of tape. Everything else is in your recycling.

This is the short version of that shopping list: one core kit, three ways to dress it, and the things you already own.

### How we chose, in four lines

- **One pick per job.** A journal, an adhesive, a pen, a tape. If a job has no good pick, we leave it empty and say so.
- **Specs from the makers, quoted as theirs.** Where a paper or glue claim appears below, it is the maker's wording, not something we measured.
- **No testing claimed.** We have not handled these products. They are chosen on maker information, which we name, and on fit with the plan.

### THE CORE KIT (the same in all three directions)

A junk journal is mostly layers, so the three jobs that matter are the base that holds them, the adhesive that fixes them, and the pen that writes on top.

**[PRODUCT BLOCK 1] The journal: Strathmore 400 Series Toned Mixed Media Hardbound Art Journal, 8.5 x 5.5 in, tan or gray**
*Job: the base that holds layers. Strathmore Artist Papers.*

This is the sturdy book the whole edit stands on. Strathmore lists the paper as 300 g/m2 (184 lb) and the binding as Smyth-sewn, which it says lets pages lie flatter; it also states the paper is acid free with 30% post-consumer fiber. The toned page gives every spread a ground colour from the start, so a sparse layout still looks considered. It suits someone who wants her paper and tape to sit on something substantial, and likes a plain cover that does not compete with what she builds. The limits: the book holds 24 sheets (48 pages by the maker's count), so it fills in a few evenings, and a toned page softens pale colours. We have no evidence on how it behaves under heavy glue, so we do not promise it will not warp. We chose it over lighter notebooks because glue and layers ask more of paper, and over other hardbounds because this is the one where the maker's own page states the paper weight and the binding.

### WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN (and the one thing we did not pick)

**Scissors: no pick.** We compared five pairs. The one we would have named is not sold through a US seller we could confirm, and the plainer alternatives did not clear our bar over what is already in your drawer. So the slot is honestly empty: use any small, sharp pair, and wipe adhesive off the blades afterwards.

**Paper to stick down, all free:** old envelopes, ticket stubs, receipts, tea-bag packets, pages from a book you do not mind cutting. Start with whatever is in your bag right now.

### IF YOU CAME FOR SCRAPBOOKING

Scrapbooking and junk journaling use the same basket. The difference is the material: printed photos and keepsakes instead of scraps. Everything above carries over. The one point that matters for photographs is the adhesive and the paper, and the claims we can pass on are the makers': Strathmore says its paper is acid free, and Tombow says its tape is "Approved Photographic Activity Test (PAT)". They are maker statements, not our testing and not conservation advice. If you are mounting irreplaceable prints, a conservator's guidance is the right source. For the hobby pages: [Scrapbooking](/hobbies/scrapbooking) and [Collage art](/hobbies/collage-art).

### LATER, NOT NOW

Not in the first basket, and no products attached: a refill cartridge for the runner (Tombow lists a separate refill SKU), a second journal when the first is full, a paper trimmer, a pen case or accordion file for storage. We have not found picks for the trimmer or storage we are ready to stand behind, so those stay empty. Also not recommended for a first basket: 200-piece sticker or ephemera bundles and 300-plus-piece "kits".

### WHAT WE LEFT OUT, AND WHY

- **Complete junk-journal kits.** We compared four. The most credible, a boxed kit from a publisher, lists a project book, a blank journal, paper sheets, washi and stickers, but we could not confirm the journal's paper weight or the washi quality, and it names no adhesive. The others were private-label bundles with unknown paper. None is in the edit; the basket above is the answer for now.
- **Larger washi assortments.** Twenty-one unbranded rolls is a drawer, not a palette.
- **A notebook built for pens, not glue.** A lovely writing notebook can be the wrong book for glue; the maker lists a lighter paper.

### FAQ

**Do I need to buy all of this?** No. A thick book, an adhesive and scraps will make a first spread. The pen and tape are what make it look finished.

**What do I do about the scissors?** Use the pair you have; small and sharp is the only brief.

**Is the adhesive safe for photographs?** Tombow describes it as acid-free and PAT-approved. That is the maker's statement. For irreplaceable prints, follow conservator guidance and test on a copy.

**Can I use a regular notebook?** Yes, to practise. A thin page may wrinkle under glue and layers, which is why this edit picks a heavy-paper journal for the keeper.

**Will the washi stick to everything?** We have not read a maker product page on adhesion. Test a strip on a scrap first.
