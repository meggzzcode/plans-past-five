## 1. Original descriptions (as in ARTICLE.md)

1. **Mont Marte clay.** The clay we would start a ring dish with, because its maker publishes how long it takes to harden, what causes cracks and a tray method built around exactly one 500 g block. Suits someone wanting a plain white ground for soft paint who would rather follow one maker's instructions end to end. Chosen over DAS because DAS publishes much less. Limits: decorative only, not food safe, evidence is maker-supplied.
2. **DAS clay.** Large white block, hardens in air with no oven; suits one block across three projects; maker says easily washable; alternative because the maker's website gives no drying or sealing advice. Not for food or drink.
3. **Galeria.** Ready-made soft shades (blush, Naples yellow, olive) close to our own palette; suits a coordinated set of three dishes; chosen over mix-your-own for that; limits: more colours than needed, small tubes, maker does not mention clay.
4. **Liquitex BASICS.** Twelve artist-brand tubes, satin; suits someone who mixes her own tints and will reuse the tubes; second because no pastels; maker's page counts colours two ways.
5. **Mont Marte sealer.** Brush-on, water-based, maker says for air-dry clay and keeps the natural look; suits a matte surface and no spray fumes; 2 to 3 even coats, soft wide synthetic brush, ventilated space; chosen over gloss, solvent and spray options because they do not mention clay; still decorative only.
