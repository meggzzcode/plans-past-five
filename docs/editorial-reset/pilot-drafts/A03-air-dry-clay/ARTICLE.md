## Body

### [Opening, no heading]

Air-dry clay needs a kitchen table, an evening and about a week. It does not need a kiln. This guide walks through three small pieces, a pinch-pot ring dish, a cutter-shaped trinket dish and a small tray, and the short list of things worth buying to make them.

The week matters more than the evening. You shape everything in one sitting, then the clay does most of the work on its own while you do something else. Expect the first dish to come out a little lopsided. A lopsided ring dish still holds a ring.

## What you need

AFFILIATE NOTE (repeat at head of shopping block): DISCLOSURE: [Amazon-required Associate statement, wording pending verification]. Our line: links below go to Amazon and we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Three purchases do the whole job: a clay, a paint set and a sealer. Everything else is in a kitchen drawer.

### Essentials

1. **Air-dry clay.** Two 500 g blocks of white clay (EDITORIAL ESTIMATE). The maker's own tray tutorial uses one 500 g block for one tray, so a single block only works if all three of your pieces stay small. Two leaves room to start over.
2. **Acrylic paint.** One set with soft tints and white. About four or five tints plus white will cover three small pieces (EDITORIAL ESTIMATE).
3. **Clay sealer.** One 120 ml bottle, brushed on in two to three even coats per piece, as the maker advises.
4. **A soft, wide synthetic brush for the sealer** and a small brush for paint. We are not recommending a specific brush: none passed our checks, so this slot is deliberately empty. Any brush you own that is soft and synthetic will do for the sealer; the maker's wording is "a soft, wide, synthetic brush".

### From your kitchen

Already own it? These stand in for tools:

- A rolling pin or a straight-sided bottle
- A butter knife and a toothpick
- A lid or a paper template, for cutting your shapes
- Cling film or parchment, to roll the clay on
- A bowl of water and a sponge
- Fine sandpaper

### Not on the list

A kit, cutters, texture mats, a pottery tool set or paint pens. We looked, and none cleared our bar for this guide, so we are not padding the list with them. The three projects work with a lid and a butter knife, which is what the clay maker's own tutorials use.

## The three pieces

Our steps follow the clay maker's published tutorials (Mont Marte), the Crayola help pages and the other makers cited. We have not made these pieces ourselves.

### One rule before you start: thickness

Makers disagree, so choose one and keep it even. Mont Marte's tray tutorial rolls the clay to 1 cm. Crayola says "at least 1/4 inch". Sculpd says at least 1/2 inch "in all places", and 3/4 inch or more for larger pieces. Quote the maker of the clay you buy. Even thickness is the point: Mont Marte says cracks "can form as air dry clay loses moisture and shrinks", and that consistent thickness and slow drying at room temperature help.

### 1. The pinch-pot ring dish

A ring dish is the smallest possible first piece, and it needs no tools at all. The clay maker shows this no-tool pinch-pot method on its own project pages.

1. Knead a piece of clay about the size of a small plum until it is pliable.
2. Roll it into a ball. Press your thumb into the middle, stopping before you reach the bottom.
3. Pinch slowly around the ball, turning it as you go, until the walls are an even thickness and the dish is shallow.
4. Smooth cracks with a fingertip that is only slightly damp. Crayola warns against too much water.
5. Set it on parchment to dry.

### 2. The cutter-shaped trinket dish

The same slab method as the maker's coloured trinket-dish tutorial, with a lid or paper template instead of a cutter.

1. Roll clay out between two sheets of cling film, as in the tutorial, to your chosen even thickness.
2. Lay a small dish, lid or paper template on top and cut around it with a butter knife. A scalloped cutter is nice but optional.
3. Lift the shape. Pinch or roll the edge upward to make a shallow curve.
4. Smooth the edge, then leave it to dry.

### 3. The ring-and-earring tray

Taken from Mont Marte's trinket-dish tutorial (8 April 2025), simplified. The maker uses one 500 g block for this alone, which is why two blocks is our default.

1. Roll about 500 g to 1 cm thick.
2. Cut an oval of roughly 22 x 12 cm from a paper template.
3. Score a border about 1 cm in from the edge, roll a thin coil and press it onto the border for a lip. Blend the join with a damp fingertip.
4. Press a pattern into the base with a toothpick, a fork or the back of a spoon.
5. The maker's instruction is to let the tray dry for 24 to 48 hours before painting.

IMAGE SLOT 6 (process, real photo only): as slot 4. Subject: hands pressing a coil onto the border of a rolled oval. Omit if not photographed.

### Paint and seal, all three

1. When the pieces are completely dry, sand any rough edge with fine sandpaper (the clay maker says its clay can be sanded).
2. Paint in thin coats with acrylic. A blush, a butter yellow and an olive make a set that sits well together on a dresser.
3. Wait at least 24 to 72 hours after painting before sealing, per the maker.
4. Brush on two to three even coats of sealer with a soft, wide synthetic brush, in a well-ventilated space. The maker says to leave a few hours between coats, but check the bottle: the Amazon listing and a stockist state a different, shorter time.

## Three common mistakes

1. **Drying it with heat.** Sculpd says not to put a piece in the oven or apply heat. Mont Marte recommends slow drying at room temperature.
2. **Uneven thickness and too much water.** Mont Marte says consistent thickness helps against cracks, and Crayola advises avoiding too much water.
3. **Painting or sealing too soon.** Paint only when the piece has dried completely, and wait 24 to 72 hours after painting before sealing.

## What it can and cannot be

A plain honest box, because this is the question people regret not asking.

- **It can be:** a ring dish, a trinket tray, a small decoration, a gift.
- **It cannot be:** food or drink ware. Mont Marte states: "While air dry clay is non-toxic, it isn't food safe." Sculpd says its clay "cannot be used to make food or drink vessels." Sculpd says not to apply heat to a piece, so we would not use one near a flame.
- **Water:** sealing does not make the clay waterproof. Crayola says sealing will not waterproof it, and Sculpd says the clay "is naturally porous and may absorb water even after drying". Keep it away from water: that is the cautious reading of what the makers say.
- **Outdoors:** makers differ on this. Crayola says to display indoors, so we do.
- **A caveat on the evidence:** the clay's statements here are the makers' own, not an independent test.

## Questions

**Do I need a kiln or an oven?**
No. This is air-dry clay and it hardens in the air. Sculpd says not to put a piece in the oven or apply heat.

**How long does it take to dry?**
It depends. Mont Marte says about 24 hours, and longer for thicker pieces. Crayola says about 24 hours dry to the touch and 72 hours completely, and that conditions and size change it. Sculpd says 24 to 48 hours. DAS gives no time online; its leaflet is the place to look.

**Why did mine crack?**
Makers point to the same causes: uneven thickness, too much water, and drying too quickly. Mont Marte: "Cracks can form as air dry clay loses moisture and shrinks."

**Can I keep rings in it?**
A ring dish is a decoration that sits on a dresser, which is the use these projects are for. Anything that holds food, drink or water is not.

**Do I have to seal it?**
Mont Marte recommends sealing as the finish, but it is not a safety step. Sealing does not make the clay food safe or waterproof. Test any sealer on a spare piece first, as Crayola suggests.

**Terracotta or white?**
Both makers offer both. No maker states a difference in smoothness, shrinkage or cracking, so we will not claim one. White takes soft paint tints. We could not find a terracotta clay we could recommend here, so the choice is white.

## Closing

Buy the clay on Friday and press the first thumb pot on Sunday. Put it on a windowsill, forget about it for a day or two, and paint it when it is dry.
