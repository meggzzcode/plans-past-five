# Research brief: creative-hobby-starter-kits-worth-considering

Status: draft. Answers below come from reasoning only. No data has been collected; every research section is pending.

1. **Why would someone search this?** A shopper deciding between a kit and loose supplies searches for 'best kits' or 'are kits worth it'.
2. **Why would someone save it on Pinterest?** A kit-versus-loose checklist graphic is a natural save for later when gift or hobby shopping.
3. **What problem or aspiration does it serve?** Problem: kit boxes promise everything and deliver little. Aspiration: buying once and getting a finished first project.
4. **What products would genuinely help?** Embroidery kits, ceramic painting kits, air-dry clay, watercolor sets and group painting kits, judged by the questions listed.
5. **What would she reasonably need to buy?** Often only one kit, or a handful of loose items. Typically about $15 to $50, to be verified.
6. **What might she already own?** Scissors, glue, brushes, a table surface.
7. **What optional products help?** Group painting kits, extra tools, sealer.
8. **What would make our recommendations better than generic shopping results?** Criteria instead of ranked names, a hobby-by-hobby verdict, red flags, and an honest admission that kits have not been hands-on tested.
9. **Which other articles should we link?** how-to-start-embroidery, how-to-start-air-dry-clay, watercolor-painting-for-beginners, junk-journaling-supplies-for-beginners.
10. **How does it contribute to long-term revenue?** High commercial intent and gift seasonality. The best hub for a later verified-product pass across several hobbies.
