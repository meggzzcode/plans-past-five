# Research brief: how-to-start-air-dry-clay

Status: draft. Answers below come from reasoning only. No data has been collected; every research section is pending.

1. **Why would someone search this?** Someone wants to try pottery-like making without a kiln or studio and searches for easy projects such as trinket dishes, jewelry holders and ornaments.
2. **Why would someone save it on Pinterest?** Finished, painted trinket dishes and ornaments photograph well, and project round-ups are a staple of craft boards. Strong save intent.
3. **What problem or aspiration does it serve?** Problem: pottery feels expensive and intimidating. Aspiration: making cute handmade things for her own shelves.
4. **What products would genuinely help?** A starter block of white clay, a mat, simple sculpting tools, acrylic paints, fine brushes and a water-based sealer, each described by what it helps her do.
5. **What would she reasonably need to buy?** Clay and a few paints. Typically about $15 to $40 in all, to be verified.
6. **What might she already own?** Rolling pin, butter knife, toothpick, jar lid, baking paper, bowl of water, baking tray.
7. **What optional products help?** Sculpting tool set, silicone mat, sanding sponge, sealer, cookie cutters.
8. **What would make our recommendations better than generic shopping results?** Honest limits (not food-safe, waterproof or candle-safe unless a label says so), four concrete projects, a drying-time plan, and troubleshooting for cracks.
9. **Which other articles should we link?** how-to-host-a-craft-night, creative-hobby-starter-kits-worth-considering, how-to-start-junk-journaling, creative-hobbies-you-can-start-at-home.
10. **How does it contribute to long-term revenue?** Evergreen, seasonal peaks around gifts and holidays. Fits a premium kit and a printable project pack.
