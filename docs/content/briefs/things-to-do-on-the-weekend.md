# Research brief: 30 Things to Do on the Weekend Instead of Wasting It

Slug: `things-to-do-on-the-weekend`. Status: draft. Written from reasoning only; no external data has been gathered.

## Why would someone search this?
It is Thursday or Friday, she has no plans and a vague dread of an empty weekend. 'Things to do this weekend' is a high-frequency, repeat-seasonal query from someone who wants options fast and a way to choose.

## Why would someone save it on Pinterest?
Thirty ideas sorted by mood is a classic saveable list. The 'one anchor, one treat, one rest' framework gives a graphic with a clear name she can pin and reuse.

## What problem or aspiration does it serve?
Problem: weekends that drift into errands and the sofa, then feel wasted. Aspiration: weekends with a small storyline and no overscheduling.

## What products would genuinely help?
A picnic-sized blanket, a daypack for walks, a board game for the social days, a small block of air-dry clay for a make-something Saturday.

## What would she reasonably need to buy?
Nothing for most items. One or two practical items if she wants to do outdoor days regularly.

## What might she already own?
Tote, notebook, thermos or bottle, blanket, comfortable shoes, cards.

## What optional products help?
Board game, daypack, picnic blanket, clay.

## What would make our recommendations better than generic shopping results?
A short method (anchor, treat, rest) before the list, ideas sorted by mood rather than by price, and a backup-plan section. Generic lists dump thirty items; we help her choose one and book it by Thursday.

## How does it contribute to long-term revenue?
A broad weekend hub that feeds the planner tool, the weekend printable and several product categories (blankets, packs, games, clay). Evergreen with recurring seasonal spikes. Good newsletter hook (a Friday weekend note). No revenue figure is claimed.
