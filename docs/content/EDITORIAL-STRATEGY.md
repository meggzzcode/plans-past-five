# Editorial strategy

Plans Past Five is pre-revenue and not yet public as of 2026-11-19 (the repo has no confirmed live domain; `site.url` in `src/config/site.ts` is a placeholder until the domain is connected). Nothing here claims traffic, rankings or earnings. Every demand or competition statement is labeled PENDING COWORK until someone checks it live.

Related: `docs/content/BRAND-VOICE.md`, `docs/content/ARTICLE-ROADMAP.md`, `docs/content/ARTICLE-QUALITY-CHECKLIST.md`, `docs/content/INTERNAL-LINKING-STRATEGY.md`, `docs/SOP-publish-article.md`.

## The commercial idea in one paragraph

The site helps women find and start hobbies outside work. Money can only come later, from affiliate links (not active; see `docs/buyer/AFFILIATE-ACTIVATION.md`) on articles where a reader is genuinely deciding what to buy. So the plan is not "write lots of listicles". It is: pick questions people actually search, answer them completely, and where a beginner needs stuff, say exactly what is essential, what is optional and what they probably already own. A reader who finishes the article should be able to start the hobby, with or without clicking anything.

## Article types

| Type | What it is | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial buying guide | "What to buy to start X", kit comparisons, supply lists with role labels (see `docs/commerce/PRODUCT-SELECTION-METHODOLOGY.md`) | Closest to a purchase decision; the main place a product button belongs |
| Shoppable beginner guide | "How to start X" with a real first project and an essentials list woven in | Teaches first, then shops; strongest for search plus Pinterest |
| Inspirational | Idea lists and plans ("things to do on the weekend") | Brand, internal-link hubs, Pinterest saves; light product use |

## The 10 questions to answer before writing each article

Write the answers in the article's research brief (`docs/content/ARTICLE-RESEARCH.md` template). If an answer is "I don't know", that is a research task, not a reason to guess.

1. **Who is this for, exactly?** Name the reader's situation (for example, someone with an hour after dinner and no supplies) and what she already owns.
2. **What is she actually trying to do?** The search intent in one sentence, and whether she wants ideas, instructions or a shopping answer.
3. **What would a complete answer include?** The questions a beginner would otherwise have to open three more tabs to answer.
4. **What does she have to buy, really?** The true essentials, the optional extras and the things most people already own. Spending nothing is a legitimate answer.
5. **What can go wrong?** Mess, cost, safety, frustration, wasted purchases. Say it plainly and early.
6. **What claims need a source?** Costs, rules, safety, availability. Each goes in `coworkChecks` until verified (`docs/SOP-publish-article.md`).
7. **What do we honestly know first-hand?** Nothing, unless the owner really did it. Never invent experience (`docs/content/BRAND-VOICE.md`).
8. **Where does she go next?** Which one or two guides, tools or printables are the natural next step, and which are not (no forced links).
9. **Which product roles does the article need?** Map each to a role label and a product record or a "type of thing to look for" description. Prices, ratings, brands and ASINs only once verified.
10. **How will she find it?** The primary keyword candidate, whether Pinterest is a likely source, and the three pin concepts (`docs/marketing/PINTEREST-STRATEGY.md`).

## What "commercial" does and does not mean here

- Does mean: clear essentials lists, role labels, honest "skip this" advice, buttons that name their real destination ("Browse options on Amazon" for a search page, "View on Amazon" only for a verified listing; see `src/config/affiliate.ts`).
- Does not mean: invented ratings, invented testing, scarcity language, price claims we have not verified, or sending every reader to buy.
- Disclosure is automatic and switches on only when an affiliate entry is live; see `docs/buyer/AFFILIATE-ACTIVATION.md`.

## Cadence and ordering (recommended, not committed)

1. Finish and verify the flagships first, then their direct spokes.
2. Publish in clusters (a hub plus two or three spokes) so internal links have real targets on day one; see `docs/content/INTERNAL-LINKING-STRATEGY.md`.
3. Do not publish an article whose `coworkChecks` are unverified. Everything else on the list stays `status: "draft"`.
