# Plans Past Five: brand voice guide

**Creative, witty best friend.** Playful, feminine, warm and clever, never childish. She is the friend who texts "okay but what are we doing this weekend" and already has three ideas.

## Core ideas (use them, do not recite them)

| Phrase | Role |
|---|---|
| Making a life, not just a living. | Tagline. Under the stacked logo, in the footer and About. |
| For the plot, not the paycheck. | Slogan. Select editorial and marketing moments. |
| Professionally off duty. | Signature phrase. Announcement strip, kickers, printable headers. |
| Sorry, I have plans past five. | The brand in one line. Social posts, merch, a pin kicker. |
| More hobbies. Fewer meetings. | Short, punchy. Banners, newsletter subject lines. |
| Let's give Saturday a storyline. | Weekend content, planner, Make Some Plans. |
| Being good at it is completely optional. | Permission slip for beginners. Any hobby intro. |
| Some things are worth being a beginner at. | Encouragement for first tries (sports, crafts, solo dates). |
| Your free time doesn't need a performance review. | Low-energy and rest content. |
| In my hobby era. / No experience, just enthusiasm. / Make plans with yourself. / Do it for the plot. | Supporting phrases from `src/config/site.ts`. |

Rule of thumb: one signature phrase per page or post. If every line is a slogan, none of them land.

## Sounds like

- Contractions. Specific everyday moments ("a Tuesday, improved", "close the laptop, open the paints").
- Gentle cheek, never sarcasm at the reader's expense.
- Concrete first, clever second: say what to do, then add the wink.
- Honest about effort, cost and time. If it is messy, say so. If it is cheap, say what "cheap" means only when verified.

## Does not sound like

- Hustle culture ("optimise your downtime", "side hustle your hobby").
- Self-improvement guilt ("you should be doing more").
- Medical or mental-health claims ("cures burnout", "reduces anxiety"). Use "gentle", "calming", "low-effort" instead.
- Fake urgency, fake scarcity, fake stats, fake testimonials, fake expertise. No "we tested 50 kits" unless someone did.
- Baby talk or cutesy spelling.

## Examples

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| Boost your productivity with leisure | Close the laptop. Open the paints. |
| Top 10 hacks for work-life balance | Ideas for when you have two hours and no desire to look at a screen. |
| You must try this life-changing hobby | Pick one. Be a beginner. Being good at it is completely optional. |
| Treat yourself, girl boss | Take yourself on a date. You're good company. |

## How the voice shows up by surface

- **Site:** short kickers, warm headlines with one italic accent word, honest "what you'll need / time / cost" blocks.
- **Pins:** the headline does the work in four seconds; the handwritten note adds the wink; the brand footer closes it. See `design/pins/`.
- **Printables:** playful headers, one signature phrase, generous white space.
- **Shop (Off Duty Essentials):** helpful, not salesy. "Where to look", never "must buy". Affiliate disclosure stays plain-spoken.
- **Tools (quiz, planner, tonight, budget):** the tool talks like a friend, not a form ("Be honest: how much energy do you have after work?").
- **Email/newsletter (when wired):** subject lines under 50 characters, one idea per send.

## Compliance guardrails

- No claims about earnings, traffic or audience. No invented reviews.
- Disclose affiliate links where they appear (`docs/buyer/` and the site's Affiliate Disclosure page).
- AI-generated imagery is disclosed in `docs/IMAGE-LICENSES.md`; do not present images as photographs of real people or real events.
